    How to interpret

    For a plane wall, Q̇ is directly proportional to thermal conductivity. A materialerial with a higher k transfers more heat when area, thickness, and temperature difference are held constant. This comparison isolates the effect of k; real equipment selection also considers strength, safety, cost, moisture, temperature limits, and chemical compatibility.

    Why this matters in Chemical Engineering

    • Energy efficiency: estimating heat loss helps evaluate insulation requirements for heated process equipment.
    • Process temperature control: heat transfer through walls affects how much energy is needed to maintain operating temperatures.
    • Material selection: conductivity and thickness influence the thermal resistance of an equipment wall.
    • Design limitation: actual vessels are often cylindrical and may involve convection, radiation, multiple layers, supports, and changing material properties. This plane-wall calculator is an educational approximation, not a complete equipment design method.

    Concept guide

    Core ideas from the ChE 122 module.

    1. What is conduction?

    Conduction is energy transfer through contact between adjacent molecules. In metals, free electrons also contribute to energy transfer.

    2. What does Fourier's law tell us?

    Heat flux is proportional to the negative temperature gradient. In one dimension, heat-transfer rate also depends on the area perpendicular to the direction of heat flow.

    q″x = −k(dT/dx)
    Q̇x = −kA(dT/dx)

    3. Thermal resistance

    For a plane wall with constant k and A:

    R = Δx/(kA)   and   Q̇ = ΔT/R

    4. Assumptions in this app

    Unless stated otherwise, the plane-wall model assumes steady state, one-dimensional heat flow, constant area, no internal heat generation, and perfect material contact.
